I dispatch airliners,
and I write software.

I’m an aircraft dispatcher at Endeavor Air. I build the flight plan, look at the weather, work out the fuel, and then I follow the flight and talk to the crew when something changes. The captain and I are both responsible for the flight once it’s out, so a lot of the job is just staying on the same page with them.

Before the airline I got a computer science degree, and I still build things on my own time. I’m also a flight instructor, CFI and CFII, which took years and a lot of checkrides. Between teaching and dispatching, most of what I do is explain complicated things to people in a way they can actually use.

I’m also working on a project that I hope changes the way flight training works. I want to make the whole thing run smoother so every student gets really good training and shows up to their airline ready.

Background

Airline operations

I’m a licensed dispatcher, which is the FAA’s ADX certificate, and I work CRJs for Endeavor. A normal shift is planning flights, reading the weather and NOTAMs, checking performance and fuel, and picking alternates. When the weather or ATC breaks the plan, I’m the one on the ground sorting out reroutes with the crew.

Software

I have a computer science degree. Most of my projects were group projects. A music captioning model in Python that we wrote from scratch, an Android app in Kotlin, a game in Godot. Hand me a codebase I’ve never seen and I’ll figure it out.

Flying & instruction

I’m a CFI, CFII, and Advanced Ground Instructor. Getting there meant working through the whole run of FAA checkrides, private up to commercial and then the instructor ratings, and learning each subject well enough to teach it. I started in high school and pretty much finished when I finished my degree.
