I dispatch airliners,
and I write software.

Right now I’m an aircraft dispatcher at Endeavor Air. I plan flights, watch the weather, run the fuel and performance numbers, and stay on the radio with crews when things change. I share operational control with the captain on every flight I release.

Before that I got a computer science degree, and I’ve kept building things on the side. I’m also a flight instructor — CFI and CFII, which took years of training and a stack of FAA checkrides to earn. A lot of my time goes into explaining how complicated systems work to the people who have to use them. I am currently working on a project that I hope will help change the way that flight training operates. I want to streamline the process and make sure that each and every student gets the best possible training and comes into the airlines well prepared.

Background

Airline operations

Certified aircraft dispatcher (FAA ADX) working CRJ flights for Endeavor Air. On a shift that means flight planning, weather and NOTAMs, aircraft performance, picking alternates and fuel, and working reroutes through New York airspace when the day falls apart.

Software

Computer science degree, plus team projects in Python (a machine-learning model we wrote from scratch), Kotlin (an Android app), and Godot. I’m fine being handed a codebase I’ve never seen and figuring it out.

Flying & instruction

CFI, CFII, and Advanced Ground Instructor. Getting there meant the whole FAA checkride ladder — private, instrument, commercial, then the instructor ratings — and learning each subject well enough to teach it. I started this process and high school, and finished it at nearly the same time as my computer science degree.
